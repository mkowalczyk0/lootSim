# Game Story & Worldbuilding — Heaven, Hell, Purgatory, and the Legends

## Core Premise

Heaven and Hell are at war.

For most of existence, the two realms have been separated from the mortal world by a great metaphysical boundary known as **the Veil**.

Different cultures have given the boundary different names:

- The Veil
- The Boundary of the Dead
- The River Between Worlds
- Purgatory

But Purgatory is not simply a waiting room for dead souls.

It is the **middle kingdom**.

The only realm positioned between Heaven and Hell.

And because of that, it is the only place capable of resisting both.

The player characters are not ordinary heroes.

They are **Legends**: figures from different eras, cultures, myths, and histories whose stories became powerful enough to leave a metaphysical imprint on the world.

The Keepers of Purgatory summon these Legends into the war because normal soldiers are no longer enough.

---

# PURGATORY

## The Third Kingdom

Heaven believes creation ultimately belongs to God.

Hell believes creation belongs to the damned.

Purgatory belongs to neither.

It is a realm of unfinished things:

- Souls who have not been judged
- Legends who have not been forgotten
- Gods whose worship has faded
- Monsters whose stories were never completed
- Broken fragments of Heaven
- Pieces of Hell
- Memories of civilizations that no longer exist
- Places and people preserved only through myth

Purgatory is essentially built from **everything that falls between absolute good and absolute evil**.

That makes it uniquely dangerous.

And uniquely important.

### Why Heaven Can't Simply Destroy It

Purgatory contains souls that Heaven cannot condemn and Hell cannot claim.

Destroying it would mean destroying the boundary separating the divine realms from mortality.

Neither side can simply wipe it out.

Instead, they fight over it.

---

# THE KEEPERS OF THE THRESHOLD

The central organization protecting Purgatory is known formally as:

> **The Custodians of the Threshold**

Most people simply call them:

> **The Keepers.**

The Keepers are not trying to defeat Heaven.

They are not trying to defeat Hell.

They are trying to prevent **either side from winning**.

Their doctrine is simple:

> **Heaven must not descend.  
> Hell must not ascend.  
> Humanity must remain free.**

The Keepers maintain the boundary between realms, close Rifts, destroy invading demons, destroy fallen celestial beings, stabilize reality, and summon Legends.

---

# THE CITADEL OF THE THRESHOLD

The Keepers operate from an enormous fortress-city constructed within Purgatory.

### The Citadel

The Citadel is an impossible city assembled over thousands of years from civilizations that died.

Roman stonework sits beside Gothic cathedrals.

Greek columns hold up ruined medieval towers.

Statues of saints stand beside pagan gods.

Ancient weapons are embedded in the walls.

Fragments of entire civilizations that should no longer exist remain preserved within its streets.

This is possible because Purgatory is shaped by **memory**.

The Citadel itself should feel like the accumulated history of humanity made physical.

---

# THE LEGENDS

## What Is a Legend?

The greatest warriors, rulers, heroes, villains, saints, monsters, and mythological figures do not truly disappear when they die.

Their bodies die.

Their kingdoms disappear.

Their names change.

Their stories become myths.

But their **legend remains**.

Every civilization leaves behind stories.

Some become so powerful that they form a **Legendary Soul** within Purgatory.

The Keepers can summon these souls back into physical form.

These beings are known as:

> **Legends.**

---

# LEGENDS ARE NOT NECESSARILY HEROES

A Legend does not have to be good.

They simply have to be **important**.

Their existence must have left enough of an imprint on history, mythology, or human memory that their legend has weight within Purgatory.

A Legend can be:

- A hero
- A tyrant
- A king
- A saint
- A heretic
- A conqueror
- A murderer
- A pirate
- A priest
- A scientist
- A warrior
- A monster
- A revolutionary
- A god
- A demigod
- A completely forgotten historical figure

The Keepers do not care whether a Legend was righteous.

They care whether their **Legend is strong enough to fight**.

---

# LEGENDS AND CLASSES

A class represents a **Legendary Archetype**, rather than necessarily being the literal identity of a character.

For example:

### Swordsman — King Arthur

Arthur Pendragon can manifest as the Swordsman.

He brings:

- Excalibur
- Arthurian techniques
- The weight of Camelot
- The myth of the Once and Future King

Another Swordsman could eventually be a completely different legendary figure, such as another famous warrior or swordsman.

This allows the game to have many Legends without requiring every new Legend to be an entirely separate gameplay class.

The same class can therefore support multiple manifestations, each with unique:

- Visual identity
- Voice
- Lore
- Weapons
- Cosmetics
- Special mechanics
- Story interactions

---

# EXAMPLES OF POSSIBLE LEGENDS

## Swordsman — Arthur Pendragon

The Once and Future King.

He failed to save Camelot.

Now he has been summoned into a war that makes his original kingdom seem insignificant.

His question is no longer whether he can save Camelot.

It is whether he can save the world.

---

## Lancer — Cu Chulainn

The Hound of Ulster.

A warrior whose legend is defined by fighting far beyond the limits of mortal endurance.

---

## Magician — Merlin

Merlin does not necessarily need to be a playable Magician.

He could instead be one of the major NPCs within the Citadel.

He understands more about the war than the Keepers realize.

This allows famous mythological figures to exist in the world without forcing all of them into the player roster.

---

# WHY THE LEGENDS ARE NEEDED

For thousands of years, Heaven and Hell fought without fully breaching the boundary.

Then something changed.

A battle occurred between two beings vastly more powerful than ordinary angels and demons.

Their clash tore through the Veil.

The aftermath created the first true **Rift**.

The war changed forever.

The Keepers realized something terrifying:

The old methods of maintaining the boundary were no longer enough.

The only beings capable of surviving and influencing the resulting chaos were the Legends.

---

# THE FIRST RIFT

The first Rift was created when two higher beings from Heaven and Hell clashed above the mortal world.

The impact tore reality apart.

The collision did not simply create a portal.

It created a **wound**.

Fragments of Heaven and Hell spilled into the mortal realm.

Ancient weapons appeared where they had never existed.

Dead creatures manifested in places where no living thing could have created them.

Pieces of celestial architecture fused with demonic landscapes.

Memories began appearing as physical locations.

Reality itself began to destabilize.

That first wound became the beginning of the current age.

---

# THE RIFTS

Rifts are not ordinary portals.

They are **wounds in reality**.

When sufficiently powerful beings from Heaven and Hell clash, the aftermath tears holes between realms.

The stronger the beings involved, the more dangerous and unstable the resulting Rift.

A Rift may contain:

- Fragments of Hell
- Fragments of Heaven
- Dead demons
- Dead angels
- Divine weapons
- Demonic artifacts
- Lost souls
- Ancient monsters
- Forgotten memories
- Strange materials
- Reality distortions

The Keepers enter Rifts to contain the damage and recover anything that could help them maintain the balance.

---

# STANDARD RIFTS

Standard Rifts are the aftermath of lesser supernatural conflicts.

Lesser angels, demons, and supernatural entities fight within reality.

Their deaths and discarded power destabilize the surrounding area.

These become the normal progression/combat environments.

---

# AVARICE RIFTS

Formerly called **Hoard Rifts**.

The new name should be:

# **Avarice Rifts**

This ties the farming-oriented gameplay to the themes of Hell, greed, and Dante-inspired cosmology.

## Lore

When lesser celestial and demonic forces battle, enormous quantities of material are left behind.

Weapons.

Gold.

Soul fragments.

Divine materials.

Demonic artifacts.

Fragments of greater beings.

Creatures swarm toward these battlefields to consume and hoard everything left behind.

The Keepers call these locations **Avarice Rifts**.

They are dangerous, chaotic battlefields where enormous quantities of valuable material can be recovered.

## Gameplay Identity

Avarice Rifts are primarily:

- High monster density
- High item quantity
- High currency quantity
- Fast-paced
- Chaotic
- Lower emphasis on extreme item rarity
- Excellent farming content

The player should immediately understand:

> **This is where I go when I want a ridiculous amount of loot and currency.**

---

# ABYSSAL RIFTS

An Abyssal Rift occurs when a Rift penetrates beyond the known layers of creation.

It does not merely breach Heaven.

It does not merely breach Hell.

It breaches whatever exists **beneath them**.

That place is known as:

# **The Abyss**

---

# THE ABYSS

Hell has structure.

Hell has circles.

Hell has rulers.

Hell has judgment.

The Abyss has none of these things.

It is older than the current structure of creation.

It represents what exists outside divine order.

Different civilizations have different names for concepts resembling it:

- Chaos
- The Void
- The Primordial Dark
- The Nothing
- The Abyss

Theologians interpret it differently.

Demons fear it.

Angels refuse to speak of it.

The Keepers barely understand it.

The Abyss is not evil in the traditional sense.

It is **unmaking**.

---

# WHY THE ABYSS IS TERRIFYING

The Abyss does not care about:

- Sin
- Virtue
- Judgment
- Heaven
- Hell
- Humanity

It erases distinctions.

A demon exposed to it can become something else.

An angel exposed to it can become something else.

A human exposed to it can become something else.

Even reality itself can become something else.

Nothing remains guaranteed to be what it once was.

---

# ABYSSAL RIFT GAMEPLAY

Abyssal Rifts are among the most dangerous forms of regular content.

They should feature:

- Higher-tier enemies
- More dangerous elite modifiers
- Extremely powerful elites
- Unique enemy behaviors
- Abyssal bosses
- Very high difficulty
- High rarity
- Abyssal-specific materials
- Artifacts

Artifacts should therefore be strongly associated with the Abyss.

They are physical remnants of things touched by forces that should not exist.

But Lucifer may not be the final villain.

---

# LUCIFER

Lucifer is imprisoned at the center of Hell.

The obvious assumption is that Heaven defeated him.

That may be wrong.

A larger possibility is that **Hell itself imprisoned Lucifer**.

Why?

Because Lucifer knows something.

He knows what is happening to the boundaries between the realms.

He knows what the Abyss is.

He knows why the Rifts are appearing.

And he knows that the war between Heaven and Hell may be destroying the thing both sides are fighting to control.

The player should never be completely certain whether Lucifer is:

- Lying
- Manipulating them
- Insane
- Telling the truth
- Trying to save creation
- Trying to save himself

This makes him a potentially recurring and important character rather than simply a final boss.

---

# HEAVEN

Heaven should not simply be "the good realm."

Heaven represents:

# **Order**

Its philosophy is that creation should be perfect.

No sin.

No suffering.

No chaos.

No deviation.

Everything has a purpose.

Everything has a place.

Everything is known.

And therefore, nothing is allowed to deviate from the divine order.

This makes Heaven just as frightening as Hell, but for very different reasons.

---

# CELESTIAL HIERARCHY

Heaven can draw from Christian angelology, Biblical imagery, apocryphal traditions, medieval cosmology, Greek mythology, and other mythological systems.

Potential celestial orders include:

### Seraphim

Burning divine beings associated with pure divine presence.

### Cherubim

Guardians and watchers.

### Thrones

Manifestations of divine law and judgment.

### Dominions

Celestial rulers and commanders.

### Virtues

Miracle-working celestial forces.

### Powers

War-oriented celestial beings.

### Principalities

Rulers over divine territories.

### Archangels

Generals and major commanders of Heaven.

Above them could exist entities that even the player cannot properly comprehend.

---

# THE THREE-SIDED COSMOLOGY

The eventual truth of the setting may be that this is not simply a war between Heaven and Hell.

There are three fundamental forces.

## HEAVEN

**Order**

> "Creation must be perfected."

## HELL

**Dominion**

> "Creation belongs to those strong enough to claim it."

## THE ABYSS

**Unmaking**

> "Creation should never have existed."

And between all three stands:

# PURGATORY

**Choice**

> "Humanity decides what comes next."

The Keepers protect that choice.

The Legends are the weapons of that choice.

---

# WHY THE LEGENDS MATTER

The Legends are more than soldiers.

They are **anchors of humanity's collective memory**.

Humanity created these legendary figures through stories.

Those stories became metaphysical identities.

When summoned, the Legends stabilize reality.

This gives the player's progression a story justification.

The player is not simply collecting XP.

They are becoming a more complete manifestation of their Legend.

---

# LEGEND MASTERY

At the beginning of a character's journey, they are only a partial manifestation of their Legend.

As they progress, they recover:

- Memories
- Techniques
- Weapons
- Relics of their original life
- Pieces of their myth
- Fragments of their identity

Their Legend becomes stronger and more complete.

Eventually they can face an endgame encounter designed to prove that they have fully realized their potential.

---

# CLASS COMPLETION

The ultimate goal for each class can be tied to the completion of its Legend.

After defeating the final endgame boss associated with that class:

> **The Legend becomes Complete.**

The class receives a:

# **Gold Border**

The gold border is not merely an achievement indicator.

It represents the character becoming a fully realized manifestation of their Legend.

This can eventually become one of the game's clearest prestige goals.

---

# RAID BOSSES

The setting allows raid bosses to become enormous mythological events.

Raid bosses do not have to simply be "large demons."

They can be manifestations of:

- Fallen celestial beings
- Ancient gods
- Mythological monsters
- War deities
- Primordial entities
- Corrupted saints
- Legendary kings
- Concepts given physical form

The game can freely draw inspiration from multiple mythologies.

---

# MYTHOLOGICAL COMPOSITES

The game does not have to reproduce mythology literally.

It can reinterpret it.

A boss can combine elements inspired by several traditions.

For example:

- Greek
- Roman
- Norse
- Mesopotamian
- Arthurian
- Christian
- Egyptian
- Japanese
- Slavic
- Completely original mythology

This allows the world to feel familiar without being constrained by any single canon.

The important rule is that every reinterpretation should still feel like it belongs to the game's cosmology.

## Rifts

General wounds caused by supernatural conflict.

**Purpose:** Standard progression and combat.

---

## Avarice Rifts

Loot-saturated battlefields created by lesser supernatural conflicts.

**Purpose:** High quantity, currency, and farming efficiency.

## Abyssal Rifts

**Purpose:** Extreme difficulty, high rarity, Artifacts, and dangerous encounters.

# THE LONG-TERM STORY

The player initially believes the mission is simple:

> Heaven and Hell are at war.

The Keepers need the Legends to survive the war.

The player fights through Rifts.

They descend into Hell.

They ascend toward Heaven.

They close increasingly dangerous breaches.

They defeat powerful angels and demons.

They become stronger.

Eventually, however, the truth begins to emerge.

The Rifts are not simply the result of the war.

The war is also **accelerating the Rifts**.

Every powerful angel that dies.

Every powerful demon that dies.

Every divine weapon destroyed.

Every soul consumed.

Every Rift opened.

Every Rift closed.

Everything is changing the structure of reality.

And all of it may be feeding something beneath creation.

---

# THE CENTRAL MYSTERY

The Keepers believe Heaven and Hell are fighting over control of the mortal world.

But perhaps they are both reacting to the same existential threat.

Perhaps Heaven wants absolute control because it believes only divine order can stop the Abyss.

Perhaps Hell wants dominion because it believes only the strongest can survive what is coming.

Perhaps the Keepers are wrong about both.

And perhaps the war itself is what will ultimately destroy reality.

The player's eventual question should become:

> **Are we supposed to win the war... or end it?**

---

**Choice**

>

>

>

# The Ashen Reliquary

The old planet system should be completely replaced.

The game no longer needs a science-fiction explanation for where crafting materials come from. Instead, the materials should come from **Purgatory itself**—from the accumulated remains of the war between Heaven and Hell.

That place is known as:

# **The Ashen Reliquary**

A vast, unnatural region of Purgatory where the remains of supernatural conflicts have accumulated over thousands of years.

It is part battlefield.

Part graveyard.

Part archive.

Part wound in reality.

And nobody fully understands how deep it goes.

---

# What Is the Ashen Reliquary?

Whenever something powerful dies in the war, it leaves something behind.

An angel does not simply leave a corpse.

A demon does not simply disappear.

A divine weapon does not become ordinary metal when it is shattered.

A god does not necessarily cease to exist when its body is destroyed.

Their essence persists.

Their blood becomes material.

Their bones become stone.

Their weapons become artifacts.

Their deaths leave behind storms, fire, frost, poison, darkness, and other supernatural remnants.

Over countless battles, all of this accumulated within Purgatory.

Eventually, those remains formed an enormous region:

> **The Ashen Reliquary.**

The Keepers discovered that these remnants can be harvested and refined.

They can be forged into weapons.

They can reinforce armor.

They can be used to reforge legendary equipment.

They can be used to maintain the Citadel.

And, most importantly:

> **They can be turned into weapons capable of killing things that should be impossible to kill.**

That is why the Keepers send the Legends into the Reliquary.

---

# Why "Reliquary"?

A reliquary is a container for the remains of something holy or important.

That idea fits perfectly.

The Ashen Reliquary is effectively a **continent-sized tomb for the supernatural**.

Inside are the remains of:

- Dead angels
- Dead demons
- Forgotten gods
- Ancient monsters
- Broken celestial weapons
- Infernal weapons
- Shattered sanctuaries
- Lost civilizations
- Ancient battlefields
- Fragments of souls
- Pieces of realms that no longer exist

The Keepers have catalogued some of it.

Most of it remains unknown.

---

# The Reliquary Is Alive

The most important thing about the Reliquary is that it should **not behave like a normal dungeon**.

Purgatory is shaped by memory.

The Reliquary is filled with thousands of years of supernatural memories.

Those memories bleed together.

As a result, the Reliquary is constantly changing.

A hallway may lead somewhere different tomorrow.

A ruined cathedral may suddenly appear where a cavern existed before.

A battlefield may materialize around the player.

A forest may be built from the memories of a civilization that died thousands of years ago.

A Greek temple may exist inside a Gothic monastery.

A celestial garden may be growing through the rib cage of some enormous dead creature.

The geography is fundamentally unstable.

This gives us a lore justification for procedural generation.

The dungeon isn't randomly generated because "that's how the game works."

It's randomly generated because:

> **The Reliquary does not remember itself the same way twice.**

---

# The Resource-Run Gameplay Loop

The player enters the Reliquary through a permanent gateway within the Citadel.

Unlike the Delve, the purpose isn't necessarily to push endlessly downward.

The purpose is **extraction**.

The basic loop becomes:

> **Enter → Explore → Fight → Harvest → Push your luck → Extract**

The deeper or more dangerous sections the player enters, the greater the potential material yield.

But materials are still unbanked loot.

Die?

You lose what you collected.

Which means the Reliquary still feeds directly into the game's central risk/reward philosophy.

The player isn't just walking around gathering rocks.

They're deciding:

> "I've got enough for what I need. Do I leave now, or do I go deeper?"

---

# Material Gathering

The old elemental materials can remain mechanically.

What changes is what they **are**.

There shouldn't be an item called:

> "Fire Material"

because that feels like a crafting placeholder.

Instead, it's something physically extracted from the world.

---

## Fire

Could be remnants of Infernal fire or celestial flame.

Possible material concepts:

**Hellfire Ember**

**Cinderheart**

**Infernal Ash**

**Ember Shard**

The important part is that the material looks like something that actually came from Hell.

---

## Cold

Frozen remnants of celestial realms or the deepest parts of Hell.

Possible concepts:

**Frostglass**

**Saint's Ice**

**Pale Crystal**

**Glacial Heart**

---

## Lightning

Fragments of divine weapons or energy released when higher beings collided.

Possible concepts:

**Divine Spark**

**Stormglass**

**Thunder Shard**

**Heaven's Fragment**

---

## Poison

The remains of corrupted life and demonic decay.

Possible concepts:

**Blight Resin**

**Venomstone**

**Rotheart**

---

## Void

Material touched directly by the Abyss.

This should be significantly rarer and more disturbing.

Possible concepts:

**Nullstone**

**Abyssal Glass**

**Black Ember**

**Void Shard**

The final names can come later.

The guiding rule should be:

> **Every material should feel like a physical piece of the mythology.**

---

# Resource Nodes

Resource nodes should stop looking like generic MMO deposits.

They should look like **things that belong in the Reliquary**.

Examples:

A sword broken off inside a stone wall, still radiating divine energy.

A giant frozen organ from some dead celestial creature.

A mass of black demonic crystal growing from a corpse.

Lightning permanently trapped inside an ancient weapon.

A pool of condensed poison dripping from the remains of a dead god.

Chunks of infernal stone still burning after thousands of years.

Bones from creatures so large the player cannot see the entire skeleton.

The player shouldn't feel like they're mining an ore vein.

They should feel like they're **harvesting the corpse of a dead god.**

---

# Reliquary Sectors

The Reliquary can eventually be divided into regions.

These aren't planets.

They're areas within the same massive supernatural repository.

Each one has its own visual identity, enemy population, events, and material affinity.

For example:

## The Cinder Catacombs

A labyrinth of ruined structures buried beneath infernal ash.

Fire-heavy.

---

## The Frozen Basilica

A massive cathedral frozen in supernatural ice.

The statues within are not statues.

They're the preserved remains of celestial beings.

Cold-heavy.

---

## The Storm Sepulcher

A battlefield where two armies of higher beings once annihilated each other.

Weapons remain embedded everywhere.

The entire region still crackles with divine lightning.

Lightning-heavy.

---

## The Rotting Garden

A beautiful celestial garden that has been corrupted by Hell.

Plants grow from corpses.

Flowers release poison.

Trees bleed.

Poison-heavy.

---

## The Black Archive

A region where the Reliquary has begun touching the Abyss.

The architecture doesn't make sense.

Rooms overlap.

Distance stops behaving normally.

Things sometimes exist where they shouldn't.

Void-heavy.

This would be one of the game's most dangerous regions.

---

## The Wargrave

An enormous battlefield packed with the remains of mortal, demonic, and celestial armies.

Broken siege weapons.

Dead horses.

Buried swords.

Armor piled in mountains.

Massive corpses half buried beneath the earth.

Physical-material focused.

---

## The Gilded Ossuary

A reliquary in the oldest sense of the word.

Saints were not buried here.

They were gilded and put on display.

The gold has kept growing since.

Holy-heavy.

---

## The Unbound Spire

A mage-tower that outlived the war it was built to win.

Its wards broke a long time ago.

Rooms rewrite themselves while you stand in them.

Arcane-heavy.

---

## The Hollow Orchard

An orchard planted over a civilization's mass grave.

It is still growing.

It is still fed.

Nature-heavy.

---

# Reliquary Events

This system gives us an opportunity to make resource farming feel less repetitive.

Sometimes the player finds something unusual.

---

## Fallen Angel

The corpse of a higher celestial being lies within the Reliquary.

Its body is surrounded by monsters feeding on its remains.

Kill the monsters and harvest the corpse.

Potentially massive celestial material payout.

---

## Infernal Breach

A temporary tear into Hell appears inside the Reliquary.

Demons begin pouring through.

The player has to survive until the breach collapses.

Then the area becomes saturated with Infernal materials.

---

## War Remnant

The player finds an ancient battlefield frozen in time.

Interacting with it causes the battle to replay.

For a short period, the player fights alongside or against echoes of the forces that originally died there.

When the event ends, the battlefield leaves behind materials.

---

## Forgotten God

A fragment of an ancient god is still alive.

The player can choose to:

Destroy it.

Harvest it.

Or attempt to kill it.

If they kill it, they receive an enormous reward.

But it's dangerous.

---

## Abyssal Contamination

A portion of the Reliquary suddenly becomes contaminated by the Abyss.

Monsters begin mutating.

Resource nodes become more dangerous.

Rare materials begin appearing.

The player can choose to push further into the contaminated zone for a much greater payout.

This can also act as the player's introduction to the Abyssal side of the world.

---

# Abyssal Reliquary Content

This is where I think the **Artifact system** can connect beautifully.

Artifacts are primarily Abyssal rewards.

The deepest or most corrupted sections of the Reliquary can contain objects that have been touched by the Abyss.

They're not necessarily weapons.

They're not necessarily divine.

They're things that **should not exist**.

And occasionally, something survives the Abyss without being destroyed.

Those are Artifacts.

So you get:

**Reliquary**

→ ordinary crafting materials

**Deep Reliquary**

→ better materials and rare events

**Abyssal Reliquary**

→ extremely rare materials + Artifacts

This creates a very natural progression.

---

# The Keepers Need the Reliquary

The Reliquary should also serve a narrative purpose beyond crafting.

The Citadel is under constant strain.

The barriers keeping Heaven and Hell from completely crossing into Purgatory are weakening.

The Keepers need resources to maintain:

- The Citadel
- The Threshold
- The wards
- Their weapons
- Their defensive structures

And the Legends themselves require materials to grow stronger.

So the Reliquary becomes essential to the war effort.

The player isn't harvesting resources because the game needs an economy.

They are harvesting them because:

> **Purgatory is running out of time.**

Every weapon forged from those materials exists because something had to die to create it.

That makes crafting feel much more meaningful.

---

# The Reliquary and the Story

This is where I think the system can eventually become **more important than just a farming zone**.

At first, the player thinks the Reliquary is basically an enormous supernatural graveyard.

Then they start finding things that don't make sense.

Old battles.

Memories.

Historical events the Keepers say never happened.

Bodies that belong to people who should have died somewhere else.

Weapons from civilizations the player has never heard of.

Eventually they realize:

> **The Reliquary isn't simply storing the remains of the war.**

It's storing the **memory of the war**.

And because Purgatory is built from memory...

the Reliquary may contain things that even the Keepers have forgotten.

Including:

> **The truth about the First Rift.**

---

# The Potential Twist

Maybe the Reliquary isn't growing because Heaven and Hell are fighting.

Maybe it has been growing because the war has happened **before**.

Again and again.

For thousands of years.

Civilization after civilization.

Legend after legend.

The same war repeating in different forms.

Different names.

Different gods.

Different armies.

But the same conflict.

The player eventually begins finding echoes of previous cycles.

And suddenly the Legends themselves become suspicious.

Why are the Keepers summoning people from so many different eras?

Why do so many myths contain references to the same war?

Why do ancient artifacts depict events that supposedly haven't happened yet?

And why does the Abyss seem to recognize the Legends?

That turns the Reliquary from:

> "The place where we farm crafting materials"

to:

> **"The graveyard containing the history of the entire war."**

---

# The Core Fantasy

The old fantasy was:

> "Travel to another planet and gather materials."

The new fantasy is:

> **"Enter the supernatural graveyard of a war between Heaven and Hell, harvest the remains of dead gods and broken realms, and forge the weapons humanity needs to survive."**

That's so much more aligned with **Ashes of Purgatory**.

And I think that title actually makes the whole resource system stronger:

### The player gathers the ashes of the war.

### The Forge turns those ashes into power.

### The Legends carry that power back into the fight.

### Eventually, the player learns what those ashes actually came from.

That gives you a repeatable farming system that doesn't feel bolted onto the setting—it feels like **one of the reasons the setting exists at all.**
